Jet Li has meditated every day for 30 years. In October 2026 he started a weekly series, *Give Me a Break!*, with his daughter Jada. This post sums up the [6-minute introductory episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vTs5EhtbrC0) and picks out what matters to people running companies.

## train the body to be healthy, the mind to be happy

He opens with a familiar arc. At 20 people want to change the world. At 30 it shrinks to Asia, at 40 the country, at 50 the city, at 60 the home. At 70 they just want to change their mind.

Few people change the world. Everyone can change how they look at it, and training the mind, he says, lets you see it wider, more peaceful and more free.

## meditation is the tool, not the goal

The line worth keeping: meditation has many styles but only one goal. That goal is **awareness**, letting the mind settle on one thing. Once you know the goal, any style will do.

Anyone working on digital transformation will recognise this. There are plenty of tools, and choosing one before the goal is clear is the surest way to waste money.

## 24 frames

As a film actor, he explains awareness with a reel of film. One second is 24 frames run together, so motion looks normal. Stop the projector and you have a single frame. The one before has passed; the one after has not happened yet. Seeing that one frame clearly and holding it is what he calls awareness.

Jada puts it in a form that is easier to use. We take in information through our senses and almost at once label it: good or bad, pretty or ugly. Awareness puts a gap between **seeing** and **judging**.

## be the boss, not the mind's employee

The body sits still while the mind has already gone off in four, five, six directions. Eating while thinking about later. Hearing a good song and following it: who wrote this? The mind, he says, is "very naughty". Meditation calls it back, because "you're the owner".

## three meanings of "give me a break"

The name comes from something he used to say on American film sets 25 years ago, trying to work out a fight while everyone around him was shouting. Father and daughter give it three meanings:

1. **Rest.** We are busy all the time with work, friends, everything. Meditation gives the mind a moment off.
2. **Cut the noise.** Let go of the noise and mess that fill so much of life.
3. **Gap.** A cut that turns 24 frames into one picture. No future, no past, only this moment. Like sitting in the dark when the lights come on and you suddenly see everything.

## what leaders should take from it

**1. Good decisions need a gap between data and judgement.** The report has barely arrived and we have already called it bad news. The meeting has barely started and we know who is right. Putting a beat between seeing and judging is a management skill, not only a spiritual one.

**2. A leader's attention is the scarcest resource.** An executive pulled six ways every hour cannot think hard about any of them. Cutting the noise can start with concrete steps: fewer chat channels, fewer meetings, and moving recurring questions to a place where others, or an AI, can find the answer themselves.

**3. Know the goal before choosing the tool.** Meditation or AI, the same holds. Many styles, many platforms, but the first question is still what the organisation wants to achieve.
